Northwoods: the war pretext that stayed on paper

A genuine military memorandum proposed manufactured grievances against Cuba.

The document proposes an airliner incident designed to make Cuba appear responsible. The plan's passengers would leave the aircraft before a substituted drone was destroyed. The outrage would be real even though the supposed attack was manufactured. This is not a reconstruction of a flight that occurred: it is an option inside an authentic 1962 military proposal.

That distinction does not make the document ordinary. A plan for deception can be historically consequential even when it remains a plan. Northwoods is disturbing because senior military officials put such options into formal writing. It needs no invented execution story to establish that fact.

01 / FOLLOWING THE STORY

After the failed invasion, the pressure continued

The Bay of Pigs invasion failed in 1961. The Kennedy administration subsequently pursued Operation Mongoose, a covert effort against Fidel Castro's government. The State Department's history places Northwoods's period within that wider campaign of pressure and planning. The proposal therefore belonged to a documented policy context, rather than appearing as an isolated fantasy without institutional connections.

Context still has limits. Mongoose was a broader programme, and its existence does not tell us which particular Northwoods options were authorised. Treating all anti-Castro activity as one undifferentiated operation would obscure the precise question: what did this memorandum recommend, who transmitted it, and did its recommendation become an order to act?

02 / FOLLOWING THE STORY

The memo turns public outrage into a planning requirement

On 13 March 1962, Joint Chiefs chairman Lyman Lemnitzer sent the Secretary of Defense a memorandum titled Justification for US Military Intervention in Cuba. It proposed incidents that could place the United States in the apparent position of responding to Cuban aggression. The options included manufactured attacks and an airliner deception using a substituted unmanned aircraft.

Other language contemplated real or simulated harm, including the sinking of a refugee boat. It would be wrong to sanitise the proposal as harmless theatre. It would also be wrong to replace the airliner scheme with a claim that its written instructions required murdering its passengers. The original pages are strong evidence precisely because their disturbing details can be read without adding new ones.

03 / FOLLOWING THE STORY

Recommendation is not authorisation

A separate contemporary record of Cuba planning preserves Kennedy's scepticism about circumstances warranting American military intervention. Its note of a 16 March meeting says no decision approving the use of American forces was expressed or implied. That helps establish the surrounding policy debate. It is not a transcript of Kennedy rejecting each Northwoods option individually.

The declassified proposal later reached the public through the Kennedy Assassination Records Review Board, and the National Security Archive published it online in 2001. The accessible record establishes a proposed deception, rather than an executed Northwoods operation. A recommendation's existence cannot stand in for operational orders, participating units or evidence that its manufactured incidents actually occurred.

SOURCE REVIEW / 9 October 2026

The proposal is authentic; the proposed operation was not executed

Northwoods documents senior military consideration of manufactured pretexts for intervention. Its status is an unexecuted proposal. The contemporary policy record supplies context about intervention planning, but should not be inflated into a word-for-word account of a presidential decision on every suggested incident.

WHAT REMAINS OPEN

The next question.

The historical force of Northwoods lies in the proposal itself and the level at which it circulated. Reducing it to a fake would discard an authentic record; converting it into an executed attack would misread that record.

It also supplies no shortcut for deciding other cases. A documented willingness to propose deception makes scrutiny reasonable, but it cannot identify the perpetrators or mechanism of a separate event.

A USEFUL NEXT TEST

For any alleged implementation, where are the authenticated authorisation, operational records and independently corroborated event evidence?
