Gimbal: the rotating image

The image turns. What turned with it?

Unidentified in the official releaseDiscuss in the Signal Room ↗Locate in the observation room ↗

The surviving observation.

Gimbal is one of the three Navy videos the Pentagon officially released in April 2020. The infrared display shows a dark, apparently flattened shape that rotates within the sensor view, accompanied by crew audio. The release authenticated the footage and characterized the phenomenon as unidentified. It did not conclude that a physical craft rotated, identify the target, or publish a definitive account of its motion. The distinction between an object's behavior and the appearance produced by a moving imaging system is central to reading this clip. The cited NAVAIR catalog supplies no useful encounter coordinates, so this record has no globe pin.

What the record establishes.

  1. 01

    The Pentagon authenticated this as a Navy video and dated the 2015 clips to January.

  2. 02

    The target's image appears to rotate within the sensor display.

  3. 03

    The release does not identify the object or endorse a specific extraordinary explanation.

Where the evidence stops.

An apparent rotation in infrared imagery is not by itself proof that a physical vehicle rotated. The public clip and its release statement do not establish distance, material, propulsion or origin.
