West Freugh: the radar echoes left unidentified

The radar report was investigated. Its conclusion did not identify the targets.

The surviving observation.

In April 1957, trailer-mounted radar units associated with RAF West Freugh’s bombing range reported unusual targets. The National Archives identifies the relevant Air Ministry records, including AIR 2/18564, AIR 20/9320 and AIR 20/9321. Its briefing describes a technical report concluding that five reflecting objects remained unidentified and that several ordinary explanations were considered unlikely. This is a strong example of a documented radar investigation reaching an unresolved conclusion. It does not independently establish the targets’ construction, exceptional performance or origin, and the globe marker shows the reporting area rather than a supposed craft position.

Where the evidence stops.

A preserved technical assessment is not proof of non-human technology or a present-day validated reconstruction of the radar returns. The checked official guides support April 1957; no exact day is asserted here without inspecting the original incident sheet. No verified official film or audio was located.
