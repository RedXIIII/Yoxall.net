A real Navy recording. A much larger witness account.

Official record
NAVAIR FLIR / DVIDS 955825
Release date
2020-04-27

Pacific Ocean off Southern California
Approximate regional marker, not an encounter coordinate

The FLIR recording belongs to the November 2004 Nimitz episode later described to Congress by former Navy commander David Fravor. A targeting sensor tracks a small area of contrast through changes in display mode and magnification. The Pentagon authenticated and officially released the Navy footage in April 2020, describing its subject as unidentified. Fravor's account of a white, elongated object and striking flight behavior is separate eyewitness testimony; he says another crew made the recording after his encounter. That distinction matters: the short video cannot independently establish every maneuver described in the wider account.

01 / THE SOURCE

A release is a record.

The war.gov PURSUE collection is an official release programme. It includes genuine sensor footage, submitted reports and material of different provenance. Publication establishes release, not every claim made in a report.

02 / THE CAMERA

Motion needs a reference.

A moving camera, zoom, glare and missing distance can change what a video seems to show. Apparent speed or shape is not a measurement until those factors are accounted for.

03 / THE ASSESSMENT

Open has several meanings.

Insufficient data, analysis in progress and disagreement are different reasons a case can remain open. Official assessments are attributed and dated; no alien probability scores are invented.
