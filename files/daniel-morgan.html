THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

Private investigator Daniel Morgan was murdered in Sydenham in 1987. Repeated investigations did not secure a murder conviction. In 2021, an independent panel criticised corrupt practices, investigative failures and institutional defensiveness, concluding that aspects of the Metropolitan Police’s conduct amounted to institutional corruption.

The panel’s task extended beyond identifying a killer. It examined whether the institution’s response itself prevented the truth from being established. Access to information and the protection of reputation became part of the history under investigation.

The Met disputed the panel’s application of institutional corruption in its response. That disagreement belongs beside the finding rather than being removed from the file. Subsequent oversight work also examined documents later found in a locked cabinet that had not been disclosed to the panel. These records concern the handling of the case; they do not establish a new murder suspect’s guilt.

Where the evidence stops.

No murder conviction followed from the panel’s report. The institutional-corruption conclusion is the panel’s finding, disputed by the Met. It should not be converted into an unsupported accusation against a particular person.
