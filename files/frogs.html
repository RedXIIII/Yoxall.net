THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

A 2010 study exposed genetically male African clawed frogs to 2.5 parts per billion of atrazine. The researchers reported that four of forty exposed animals developed as functional females, with mating and viable eggs observed. Other exposed males showed impaired reproductive measures.

That is a published finding about sexual development and reproduction, not proof that chemicals “turn frogs gay.” EPA’s reviews and other studies disputed whether gonadal effects were consistent and reproducible. The striking experiment deserves examination alongside those disagreements. It does not establish a government plot, human sexual-orientation changes, or that Alex Jones’s broader claim was confirmed.

The meme compresses several biological claims into one phrase. The featured experiment examined genetically male African clawed frogs and reported changes in sexual development and reproductive function. That is not the same variable as sexual orientation, and a finding in frogs does not establish a corresponding effect in humans.

A striking result gains strength through replication, methods scrutiny and agreement across appropriate studies. Here, the disagreement is part of the scientific record. Read the original experiment and the historical EPA review together, keeping the reported effect, its reproducibility and the broader online narrative separate.
