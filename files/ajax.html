THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

Released US records document planning and implementation around the 1953 overthrow of Iranian prime minister Mohammad Mosaddeq. They show British approaches to the United States in late 1952 and the subsequent covert operation. The 2017 retrospective Foreign Relations volume filled major omissions in the earlier official documentary account.

The history did not begin with declassification. Participants and writers had described the operation much earlier. What changed was the ability to compare claims with officially released records, including communications that had previously been withheld. “Newly acknowledged” should not be confused with “never previously reported”.

Foreign involvement is established, but an explanation of the coup must also account for Iranian actors and political conditions. Covert planning did not make everyone inside Iran a puppet, nor does one documented intervention prove every later regional event was centrally controlled. The specific operational trail is powerful enough.

Where the evidence stops.

The files establish foreign covert involvement, not an exhaustive account of every Iranian participant or a universal explanation for later politics. British involvement is labelled separately from events in England.
