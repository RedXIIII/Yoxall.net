THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

The disaster at Hillsborough on 15 April 1989 killed 97 people. Claims blaming Liverpool supporters persisted while families challenged the official handling. The 2012 Independent Panel found supporters were not responsible for the disaster and exposed alterations to police statements. The IOPC records the central failure as police control.

Compare the original recollections with their edited versions. The panel identified removals that weakened accounts of management and communications failures while retaining hostile descriptions of the crowd. The important evidence is not only that wording changed, but what changed and whose account benefited.

Hillsborough also shows the difference between truth established in a report and outcomes in criminal proceedings. An inquiry, an inquest and a trial have different tasks and standards. The enduring record of failure and misleading narratives should be described precisely, without treating every subsequent legal question as resolved by the same finding.
