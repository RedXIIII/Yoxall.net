THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

At a 1975 Senate hearing, CIA director William Colby displayed an electrically operated dart weapon. He described its small projectile and near-silent firing. Documents discussed developing a device for covert delivery of biological or chemical agents, alongside the stockpiling of lethal and incapacitating materials.

The weapon and research programme were real. The hearing also examined toxins retained despite orders to destroy them. Colby said he knew of experiments but no record of the gun’s actual operational use. The testimony does not establish a deployed, invariably undetectable “heart attack gun” or identify people killed with it.

The Senate demonstration gave a physical object to something that would otherwise sound like a thriller. A device could fire a small projectile quietly, and agency research considered covert delivery of dangerous agents. The committee’s task was to connect the object to programmes, retained materials and decisions.

Popular retellings often add a particular physiological effect, perfect invisibility and a list of alleged victims. Those are separate claims. Colby’s testimony acknowledged experimentation while denying knowledge of a record of operational use. A displayed weapon establishes capability research, rather than a proven history of specific assassinations.

Where the evidence stops.

Undetectability was a design objective and a claim under questioning. The hearing did not establish lethal operational deployment.
