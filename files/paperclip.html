THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

After the Second World War, US programmes recruited German and Austrian scientists and technicians. Paperclip brought military expertise into American projects despite serious concerns about some recruits’ Nazi affiliations. National Archives records describe requests for revised security reports after officials rejected particular admissions.

A recruitment policy and its implementation can diverge. Truman’s conditions sought to exclude active supporters of Nazism, while military agencies pressed for people they considered valuable. The archive exposes the handling of that contradiction, rather than leaving the story at a list of famous scientists.

Some recruits had been members of Nazi organisations; individual wartime responsibility still requires case-specific evidence. Party membership, participation in forced-labour programmes and a proved war crime are different findings. The disturbing institutional choices become clearer when those distinctions are kept.

The programme does not establish that every recruited scientist committed a war crime. Individual histories and the institutions’ choices must be examined separately.
