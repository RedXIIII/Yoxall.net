THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

SHAMROCK was a long-running arrangement in which the NSA and its predecessors received copies of international telegram traffic leaving the United States. The Church Committee exposed it as part of the investigation of intelligence activity. Participating companies supplied material through an arrangement largely invisible to ordinary senders.

This is a surveillance story before social networks and smartphones. The infrastructure carrying a message also offered an opportunity to copy it. Access depended on relationships with companies, not only on the interception technology imagined in popular spy stories.

The record distinguishes companies and periods rather than claiming every telegram in every direction was read. Copies supplied, messages selected and messages actually reviewed are different measures. That same distinction remains important when modern collection totals are presented as though every item received a human analyst’s attention.

Where the evidence stops.

Receipt of traffic is not the same as reading every individual message. The arrangement’s existence does not establish that a particular person’s telegram was selected or examined.
