THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

The Information Commissioner investigated the use of data analytics in political campaigns, including Cambridge Analytica, Global Science Research and Facebook data. The 2018 report traces data flows, consent problems and relationships among organisations. It provides an evidential basis for concerns that had sounded like speculative accounts of invisible manipulation.

The strongest question is concrete: which data were obtained, under what permission, passed to whom and used how? The regulatory investigation moves from the advertising pitch to those traceable actions. A person’s social-media information can travel further than the interaction that appeared to collect it.

Claims of uniquely powerful psychological control are harder to establish than improper data handling. Campaign outcomes depend on many forces. An archive should not mistake a company’s sales pitch for an independently measured effect, or assume that any one service determined an election.

Where the evidence stops.

The investigation does not prove that psychographic targeting controlled voters or single-handedly decided an election. Allegations, regulatory findings and company claims must remain distinguishable.
