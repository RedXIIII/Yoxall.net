THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

The June 1972 break-in at Democratic National Committee headquarters led to a wider investigation into political espionage and a White House cover-up. Nixon’s recordings became decisive evidence. A June 23 conversation showed a plan to use the CIA to obstruct the FBI investigation under a misleading national-security explanation.

The burglaries and the concealment are related but distinct parts of the record. An explanation that treats a covert political operation as a security matter can redirect institutional scrutiny. The surviving tape allows the strategy to be examined in the participants’ own conversation.

The archive also warns about transcripts. The National Archives treats a transcription as an interpretation of the recording, vulnerable to hearing errors. The original medium remains important. Watergate’s evidential force lies in multiple records converging, rather than in every later rumour about missing minutes.

The established record.

  1. 01

    Five burglars were arrested on 17 June 1972.

  2. 02

    The existence of the White House recording system was revealed in 1973.

  3. 03

    Nixon resigned in August 1974 after the tapes and investigations.

How the story unfolded.

  1. 17 June 1972

    The burglars are arrested

    The DNC break-in is discovered.

  2. 1973

    The tapes become known

    The investigation learns of the White House recording system.

  3. 1974

    The evidence forces the issue

    The Supreme Court orders surrender of tapes; Nixon resigns.

Where the evidence stops.

The record establishes the cover-up; it does not verify every theory about the erased 18½ minutes or settle every person’s prior knowledge of the initial burglary.
