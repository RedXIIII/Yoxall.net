THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

British planners placed false military documents on a dead body and arranged for it to be found off the Spanish coast in 1943. The purpose was to mislead German intelligence about the intended Allied invasion. The papers pointed attention toward other destinations while the actual target was Sicily.

The deception needed more than a false letter. The invented officer had to seem like a person whose papers could be believed. Context, identity and the circumstances of discovery helped make the manufactured account look like an accidental disclosure.

Mincemeat belongs in the archive as a real secret operation, rather than a claim that it began as a widely dismissed conspiracy theory. It also provides a comparison for Northwoods: here there is evidence of execution, while Northwoods remains a proposal. Similarity in the use of deception does not make the cases equivalent.
