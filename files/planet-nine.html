THE STORY BEHIND THE HEADLINE

Planet Nine is a proposed explanation for aspects of the distant Solar System’s orbital structure. It has not been directly detected. In July 2025, Subaru researchers announced the distant object Ammonite, whose orbit does not align with other sednoids. Their analysis adds constraints and challenges to some versions of the hypothesis, while leaving room for alternative histories and different distant-planet models.

The appeal of Planet Nine is a familiar scientific move: infer an unseen cause from its possible effects. The difficulty is that the observed distant-object population is small, and discovery depends on where telescopes looked and what they could detect. A proposed planet must explain the pattern after those selection effects are considered and make predictions that new discoveries can test. A simulation that reproduces some orbits is useful evidence about a model, rather than a photograph of its proposed planet.

Ammonite, officially 2023 KQ14, gives that argument a new object to explain. Subaru’s 2025 announcement says its current orbit does not cluster with the other sednoids; the accompanying research reports a stable orbit and suggests that some hypothetical planets would need more distant orbits. The team discusses possible early Solar System histories as well. Ammonite neither discovers Planet Nine nor excludes every possible planet. It makes a simple, universal clustering story harder to maintain.

  2. 2023–2024

  3. 14 July 2025

    A new constraint becomes public

    The discovery and dynamical analysis are announced; Ammonite does not share the other sednoids’ present alignment.

Where the evidence stops.

Indirect dynamical arguments do not establish a discovered planet. One contrary orbit challenges particular models, rather than ruling out every distant planet; simulations depend on assumptions and survey selection.

What would move this forward?

  • Detect a consistent moving source and determine an orbit matching a specific planetary prediction.
  • Model survey selection before assessing whether orbital clustering is statistically compelling.
  • Test whether candidate models reproduce Ammonite and the wider observed population together.
