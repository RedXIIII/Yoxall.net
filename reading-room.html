How to read the evidence.

The archive includes secrets, discoveries and unresolved questions. Some were once dismissed; others were always known within a small circle. These histories earn their place through the evidence, rather than a claim that every one began as a conspiracy theory.

Documented programme

An authentic record establishes a programme existed. Its existence does not establish the effectiveness of every technique attempted.

Court finding

A judgment establishes what the court found in a particular proceeding. Civil findings, convictions and acquittals answer different questions.

Inquiry finding

A panel reached a published conclusion after examining evidence. That conclusion is attributed to the inquiry; it is not silently converted into a criminal conviction.

Regulatory settlement

A regulator’s allegations and an agreed order establish the enforcement record. Settlements may have no admission of liability.

Archaeological find

A surviving object or site is established. Identity, use, meaning and individual construction methods may still be debated.

Strongly supported hypothesis

Several observations converge on a specific explanation, but the decisive direct test may still be missing.

Plausible / unresolved

Evidence justifies investigation. “Not excluded” alone is weaker than positive evidence for an explanation.

Contested finding

A published result faces material objections or incomplete replication. Editorial notices and contrary evidence are part of the file.

HOW WE USE “LIKELY”

Probability belongs to an exact claim. A hidden ocean can be strongly supported while life in that ocean remains unconfirmed. A measured void does not identify its contents. A public inquiry does not pre-judge its outcome. No numerical confidence scores are invented for this archive.
